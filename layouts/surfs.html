How it works

Turn the crown. Go surfing.

Made for wet hands and cold water: the Digital Crown starts and finishes a surf, and Water Lock keeps the screen out of it in between.

  1. {{ partial "device.html" (dict "name" "watch-start" "width" 240 "alt" "StokeRanker’s start screen on Apple Watch at Emerald Beach: a ring around a surfer and Start, with Turn the crown to start.") }}

    1 Start on the sand.

    One full turn of the crown starts the surf. It picks up the break you were looking at on your iPhone, and Water Lock comes on.

  2. {{ partial "device.html" (dict "name" "watch-lastwave" "width" 240 "alt" "During a surf: the last wave was 76 metres long at 15.6 km/h, with 19 seconds riding.") }}

    2 Surf. It keeps count.

    Each ride is picked up from the Watch’s GPS. A glance shows your time in the water, your waves, and the length and top speed of the last one.

  3. {{ partial "device.html" (dict "name" "watch-summary" "width" 240 "alt" "After the surf: Surf saved, 56 minutes 46 in the water, 8 waves.") }}

    3 Finish and pick your board.

    Unlock, turn the crown two full turns, and choose the board you rode. The surf heads to your iPhone as soon as it’s in range.

Started by mistake? A start and stop with no waves and hardly any movement asks whether to keep it.

Your surfs

Every ride, on the map.

Each surf gets a card with its rides drawn on satellite imagery of the beach. Open one for numbered rides, the longest ride and top speed, how long you were out, and the board you rode.

  • The forecast for that break and morning, beside what you surfed.
  • Rate the waves, and see how the score compares.
  • Your own spots, for places that aren’t on the forecast.
  • A weekly strip of surf days, and your streak.

Private

Your surf log, across your devices.

Your surfs, spots and boards sync between your iPhone and iPad through your private iCloud storage. We can’t see your private surf log.

Apple Health

A workout, if you want one.

Allow it, and each surf is saved to Apple Health as a surfing workout with its route, heart rate, and energy. Say no, and the surf is still recorded.

Better forecasts

Your surfs check the forecast.

Every rated surf shows what the forecast said for that morning, so you can see which model got it right. If you share wave ratings to help improve forecasts, those are sent separately. Shared ratings aren’t attached to your name or account, so we don’t know who sent them.

Screenshots show invented example surfs. Recording surfs needs an Apple Watch with watchOS 26 or later, paired with an iPhone running iOS 26 or later.
