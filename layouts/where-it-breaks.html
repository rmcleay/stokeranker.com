The physics

Real wave physics, on the real seabed.

A swell forecast tells you what’s arriving out to sea. StokeRanker follows each wave in to the sand with refraction ray tracing, a standard tool of coastal engineering, run over an airborne laser survey of every break’s seabed. It’s the same physics that makes a point break work, run for every break, every hour.

  1. 1

    Start with the forecast swell.

    Each hour’s main swell, its period and the direction it comes from, is taken from the marine forecast, out in deep water.

  2. 2

    Trace it over the seabed.

    Wave rays are traced in across a 5 m grid of the NSW Marine LiDAR seabed survey. Waves slow down in shallow water, so they bend towards banks and reefs, growing where they bunch up and shrinking where they spread out.

  3. 3

    Find where it breaks.

    Where the water gets shallow enough, the waves break. A crest that meets the bank at an angle peels; one that hits it square on closes out. The map marks both, then picks the take-off and the section you’d ride.

Reading the map

Every line means something.

  • White lines wave crests, one wave period apart
  • Orange breaks and closes out
  • Bluish green breaks and peels
  • Yellow the section you’d ride
  • Dashed line where the biggest sets break, about 1 in 100 waves
  • Thin line where half the waves have broken
  • Red ring the take-off the score is worked out for
  • ☆Star the first surfable section the map finds

The compass underneath shows the swell in blue, chop in grey, and the wind in green.

Honest about it

A forecast, not a photo.

Sand moves

Banks shift.

The seabed survey is from 2018. Sandbanks move after every big swell, so a bank on the map may have shifted since.

Simplified

Some things it can’t see.

It doesn’t model waves wrapping into the lee of a headland, or currents, so sheltered corners can show less than turns up.

Checked

Held up against the beach.

Photos taken on the beach are marked up and compared with the maps, and the width of the surf zone is checked against satellite passes.
